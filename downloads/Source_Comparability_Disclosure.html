Source Comparability Disclosure

Last updated: 2026-05-13

This disclosure is written for external readers of the atlas and release dataset. It explains where the project inputs are directly comparable, where they are only comparable within a source family, and where the project uses labelled extension sources rather than the core baseline source stack.

This note records whether the project’s core inputs are comparable enough to support the published LI, ILI, HLI, and IHLI indicators. It focuses on measurement characteristics that can change interpretation even when formulas are implemented correctly: monetary units, price-year treatment, inequality concepts, health-expectancy definitions, and observed versus modelled evidence.

The short conclusion is:

  • National LI and HLI monetary inputs are on the strongest footing because national GDPpc uses World Bank WDI NY.GDP.PCAP.PP.KD, which is GDP per capita in constant 2021 international dollars adjusted by purchasing power parity.
  • Subnational monetary inputs are not all the same currency/price-family. EU NUTS2 uses Eurostat PPS, OECD regional layers use OECD regional PPP-style units, China uses NBS/GDL source-family inputs, Mexico currently uses a GDL income proxy, Argentina uses constant-2004-peso PBG per capita, and Sri Lanka uses nominal monthly household income.
  • Gini inputs are numerically standardized to 0-1, but their survey concepts differ. This is why the project keeps Core baseline, Extended Gini, and regional-source provenance separate.
  • WHO HALE and IHME/GBD HALE should not be described as the same evidence class. WHO GHO remains the core baseline HALE source; IHME/GBD is accepted as a clearly labelled modelled extension.

Current Formula Implications

The indicators inherit the measurement unit of the component inputs.

indicator formula inherited unit issue
LI GDPpc * LEXP Monetary unit of GDPpc multiplied by years of life expectancy.
ILI GDPpc * LEXP * (1 - Gini) Same monetary-life unit as LI, adjusted by a unitless inequality factor.
HLI GDPpc * HALE Monetary unit of GDPpc multiplied by healthy-life-expectancy years.
IHLI GDPpc * HALE * (1 - Gini) Same monetary-healthy-life unit as HLI, adjusted by a unitless inequality factor.

This means inflation and purchasing-power treatment matter most for GDPpc. If a GDPpc input is current-price or local-currency, LI and HLI can be distorted by inflation or exchange-rate changes. If a GDPpc input is constant PPP, it is much better suited for comparisons over time and across countries.

National Source Comparability

GDPpc

Current national input:

  • Pipeline artifact: data/raw/downloads/gdppc_ppp_constant_1990_2024_all.csv
  • Pipeline source code: scripts/bootstrap_worldbank_inputs.sh
  • WDI code: NY.GDP.PCAP.PP.KD
  • World Bank label in the downloaded artifact: GDP per capita, PPP (constant 2021 international $)
  • Source link: https://databank.worldbank.org/metadataglossary/world-development-indicators/series/NY.GDP.PCAP.PP.KD

Decision:

  • Treat the national monetary spine as inflation-adjusted and PPP-adjusted.
  • Interpret national LI, ILI, HLI, and IHLI as using constant 2021 international-dollar GDPpc units.
  • Do not mix national values with subnational values from other monetary source-families without an explicit comparability warning.

Remaining caveat:

  • A change in World Bank PPP benchmark year can revise historical levels. The project should keep the WDI code and downloaded file headers in the release dataset so users can see which benchmark was used.

LEXP

Current national input:

  • Pipeline artifact: data/raw/downloads/lexp_1990_2024_all.csv
  • WDI code: SP.DYN.LE00.IN
  • World Bank label in the downloaded artifact: Life expectancy at birth, total (years)
  • Source link: https://databank.worldbank.org/metadataglossary/world-development-indicators/series/SP.DYN.LE00.IN

Decision:

  • Treat national LEXP as life expectancy at birth, total population.
  • It is conceptually aligned with the paper formula and with the current website labels.

Remaining caveat:

  • Life expectancy is a period life-table measure, not a forecast of a real newborn cohort. This is standard and should not be treated as a project flaw, but the Methods page should keep “at birth” language clear.

Gini

Current core national input:

  • Pipeline artifact: data/raw/downloads/gini_1990_2024_all.csv
  • WDI code: SI.POV.GINI
  • World Bank label in the downloaded artifact: Gini index
  • Source link: https://databank.worldbank.org/metadataglossary/world-development-indicators/series/SI.POV.GINI

Decision:

  • Treat World Bank WDI Gini as the core baseline inequality input.
  • Normalize all Gini values to the 0-1 scale before applying (1 - Gini).
  • Keep the World Bank caveat visible: WDI Gini can be based on income or, in some countries, consumption expenditure.

Remaining caveat:

  • Gini values are unitless but not automatically concept-identical. Income Gini, consumption Gini, disposable-income Gini, equivalised-household-income Gini, and modelled disposable-income Gini can differ materially.

HALE

Current core national input:

  • Pipeline artifact: data/raw/downloads/hale_who_1990_2024_all.csv
  • WHO GHO indicator: WHOSIS_000002
  • Pipeline filter: Dim1=SEX_BTSX and SpatialDimType=COUNTRY
  • Source link: https://www.who.int/data/gho/data/indicators/indicator-details/GHO/gho-ghe-hale-healthy-life-expectancy-at-birth
  • WHO metadata link: https://www.who.int/data/gho/indicator-metadata-registry/imr-details/7752

Decision:

  • Treat WHO GHO HALE as the core baseline HALE source.
  • Treat it as healthy life expectancy at birth, both sexes, in years.
  • Preserve the split publication policy: core HLI/IHLI cannot extend past the available WHO HALE years without an explicit extension source.

Remaining caveat:

  • WHO HALE is an estimated summary measure and is not the same as raw observed mortality. WHO metadata notes method revisions over time; cross-release comparisons should therefore keep the downloaded artifact date and indicator code visible.

Extended National Tracks

Extended Gini

Current implementation:

  • Source fusion script: scripts/fuse_gini_sources.py
  • Stage B runner: scripts/run_stage_b_multisource_extension.sh
  • Rule: first non-null source by explicit priority.
  • Typical source concepts in the runner:
    • wb_income_or_consumption
    • wiid_disposable_income
    • wid_disposable_income
    • oecd_idd_disposable_income
    • swiid_disposable_income

Decision:

  • Extended Gini improves computability, not conceptual purity.
  • The extension is acceptable because it is separated from the core baseline and because row-level source labels are kept in details/audit files.
  • The website and release dataset must not describe Extended Gini as if every row used one identical survey concept.

Recommended website wording:

Extended Gini adds additional inequality sources where the core World Bank Gini is missing; source concepts can differ and are carried in provenance fields.

Extended Gini + Extended HALE

Current implementation:

  • Builder: scripts/build_ihme_national_hale_track_v2.py
  • Source bundle: ihme_gbd_2023_national_hale_modelled
  • Quality label: ihme_modelled_hale_age_id_22_all_ages_metric_years_national
  • Source link: https://vizhub.healthdata.org/gbd-results/

Decision:

  • IHME/GBD HALE is accepted as a modelled HALE extension, not as a replacement for WHO GHO HALE.
  • It belongs in the Extended Gini + Extended HALE track.
  • It should improve coverage only where the conservative WB ISO3 to IHME bridge maps unambiguously.

Remaining caveat:

  • The current IHME export is labelled age_id 22 / All Ages, sex Both, metric Years. IHME publications report HALE as a life-expectancy-at-birth summary measure, while the Results Tool export labels the age field as All Ages. The project therefore describes this as IHME/GBD modelled HALE, exported with age All Ages and interpreted as HALE at birth following GBD reporting conventions; the exact source label remains visible.

Subnational Source Comparability

Subnational outputs should be interpreted as source-family regional evidence, not as one perfectly harmonized global regional panel.

Important consequence:

  • It is acceptable to compare regions within a regional dataset, and often within a common source family such as EU NUTS2.
  • It is not yet methodologically clean to rank every subnational region in the world as if all GDPpc and Gini concepts were identical.

Geography Bridge Disclosure

The atlas distinguishes a missing source from a geography mismatch. A source is not discarded merely because its region names or boundary level differ from the current atlas layer, but it is also not used unless the bridge can be explained and reproduced.

The project currently allows these geography bridges:

  • Exact region-code joins, such as NUTS2 IDs or official province/district codes.
  • Name or alias joins where the regions are the same but labels differ because of spelling, language, abbreviations, or diacritics.
  • Aggregation from smaller source units to a broader displayed region, ideally with weights when the source concept requires them.
  • Assignment from a broader but still genuinely subnational source region to its documented component regions, with a visible geography-proxy label.
  • Separate source-specific geography layers when a useful source does not match an existing atlas geography.

The project does not allow national-to-regional fanout in the main subnational atlas. A national Gini, national HALE, or national life expectancy value is not copied to all provinces, states, districts, or regions simply to fill gaps. If a future sensitivity exercise uses national values this way, it must be released as a separate experimental approximation product rather than as observed or modelled subnational evidence.

Examples:

  • Saudi Arabia is mapped through five genuine GDL macro-regions built from documented component administrative regions. This is a grouped subnational bridge, not national fanout.
  • Thailand uses province-level HALE and province-level income Gini grouped to five GDL macro-regions. The Gini bridge is an unweighted province mean and is therefore labelled as a grouped subnational proxy, not a recomputed macro-region distribution.
  • Belgium uses Sciensano healthy life years at age 15 as a labelled HLE proxy, not at-birth HALE. Flanders and Wallonia are broader source regions assigned to component NUTS2 provinces, while Brussels is exact.
  • France and Spain include labelled cases where a broader or combined regional source value is assigned to component NUTS2 rows because the source reports a real subnational combined unit.
  • Argentina and Sri Lanka use region-code joins for the implemented province and district datasets.
  • Broad GDL SHDI regional datasets remain their own geography layers when they do not match another atlas source family.

Specific Risks And Current Mitigations

Risk 1: inflation or price-year mismatch in lifetime income

Assessment:

  • National tracks: handled well by World Bank constant 2021 PPP GDPpc.
  • EU NUTS2: handled within Eurostat PPS source-family, but not the same unit as World Bank constant 2021 international dollars.
  • Mexico GDL: not official GDPpc; it is an income proxy.
  • China and other regional layers: source-family notes remain necessary.

Mitigation:

  • Keep national and subnational comparability caveats visible.
  • Add a Methods note that LI values inherit the GDPpc source unit.
  • Preserve source bundle, method flag, and quality flag columns in downloads.

Risk 2: Gini concept mismatch

Assessment:

  • Numerically standardized: yes.
  • Conceptually harmonized across all sources: no.

Mitigation:

  • Keep Core baseline and Extended Gini separate.
  • Keep subnational overlay source labels visible.
  • Avoid describing Extended Gini as an official harmonized inequality panel.
  • Germany and Netherlands now add official observed regional Gini overlays, but their concepts remain source-specific: Germany uses the Statistikportal Mikrozensus Land income-distribution Gini, while the Netherlands uses CBS VSO province Gini for standardized disposable income of private households excluding student households. Germany NUTS2 rows using broader Land values are labelled as geography proxies.

Risk 3: HALE source and age-definition confusion

Assessment:

  • WHO GHO core HALE is clearly HALE at birth.
  • IHME/GBD HALE is modelled and labelled with GBD export fields.
  • OWID is not currently a production replacement; it is a validation mirror for WHO HALE.

Mitigation:

  • Keep WHO HALE in the core baseline.
  • Keep IHME/GBD HALE only in the labelled Extended HALE lane.
  • On the website, say “modelled IHME/GBD HALE” rather than simply “extra HALE.”

Website Disclosure Decision

The website should include a short Methods panel with these points:

  1. National LI/HLI use World Bank constant 2021 PPP GDPpc, so national monetary comparisons are inflation- and purchasing-power-adjusted.
  2. Regional LI/HLI inherit the monetary unit of their source family; EU NUTS2, OECD TL2, GDL, NBS, and national-stat-office lanes are source-family comparable, not one globally harmonized regional currency panel.
  3. Gini values are scaled consistently, but source concepts differ. Extended Gini and subnational overlays therefore remain labelled.
  4. WHO HALE is the core baseline health input; IHME/GBD HALE is a clearly labelled modelled extension.
